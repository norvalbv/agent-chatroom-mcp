\section{What this does not show}
\label{sec:limits}

The three fixed tasks were chosen for known, single-trap failure modes after pilot screening. Repeated seeds measure variation on those task instances; the two \texttt{stamp} programs are one family. Their observed minority-versus-majority answer pattern is a specific diagnostic for the vote selector, not a rule for multi-agent systems. Study 1's direct C--K tests lack the precision to establish equal accuracy. The planned vote-versus-chatroom comparison under one stable serving condition was never run at adequate power.


The different Study 1 collection windows are relevant even though the logged account switch came after K: clock time alone does not guarantee every unrecorded serving detail was fixed. The evidence warrants a direct comparison of the observed pass counts, with uncertainty, rather than a claim that the two protocols were exposed to identical hidden conditions. Conversely, the superseded early shift estimate is not evidence that K crossed the logged account boundary.

The maintainer's working pipeline---prompt, prior-art agent, plan, build, and devkit review---has never been compared with one agent, a builder--reviewer pair, or a room. The marginal value of review by a non-author was not identified either. B ran in Study 2 and in a small pilot, but the Study 2 primary was essentially absent. A separate build-suite diagnostic reached a 9/9 ceiling in every cell. The Study 1 seat-summary tallies report corrections after disagreement; they are self-reports and cannot separate review from other room activity.

The bundled chatroom build was compared with other arms, but its individual mechanics were not ablated. Regression tests show that gates and assignments operate as coded; one bundled-build trial is not a causal estimate for a gate. The retained room artifacts also lack the event sequence needed to reconstruct which action corrected an answer. No effect size is assigned here to blind openings, challenge, amended proposals, quote-checked votes, reviewer assignment, or verification.

Other engineering observations are outside the task-success comparison. Held waits were measured at 115.6 million versus 53.6 million tokens in one room each on different tasks, so that contrast is observational. OpenRouter sticky-session caching was mock-tested only. Checkpoint trimming has no answer-quality test. None of these results supports a claim about the measured A, B, C, or K accuracy differences.

The active account and effort settings were not randomized. The account association does not reveal whether a plan tier, provider-side experiment, or another serving condition produced the different thinking-token distributions. The measured pass rates, costs, and ceiling on \texttt{stamp-interpreter} do not transfer to newer models. What can transfer is the comparison method and the practice of recording model, account, time, and per-run telemetry together. The observed answer-cluster pattern remains a hypothesis for a new prospective test.
