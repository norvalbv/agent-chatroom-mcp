\section{What this does not show}
\label{sec:limits}

The planned vote-versus-chatroom comparison under one stable serving condition was never run at adequate power.



The maintainer's working pipeline---prompt, prior-art agent, plan, build, and devkit review---has never been compared with one agent, a builder--reviewer pair, or a room. The marginal value of review by a non-author was not identified either. B ran in Study 2 and in a small pilot, but the Study 2 primary was essentially absent. A separate build-suite diagnostic reached a 9/9 ceiling in every cell.

The bundled chatroom build was compared with other arms, but its individual mechanics were not ablated. Regression tests show that gates and assignments operate as coded; one bundled-build trial is not a causal estimate for a gate. No effect size is assigned here to blind openings, challenge, amended proposals, quote-checked votes, reviewer assignment, or verification.

The review audit (Section~\ref{sec:results-review}) is post hoc and was not pre-registered. Its census covers structured verification entries only, and its classes come from AI coders reading what each entry says was run, not from observed execution; the two coders share a model, and an independent review of 48 entries found three labels both had got wrong, which are corrected. Its second measure rests on AI auditors whose counts moved on re-count and whose labels include mixed methods and disputed findings. Its rooms mix models and projects and were run by one operator. It describes recorded verification practice; it does not establish what a different rule would have caught.

Other engineering observations are outside the task-success comparison. Held waits were measured at 115.6 million versus 53.6 million tokens in one room each on different tasks, so that contrast is observational. OpenRouter sticky-session caching was mock-tested only. Checkpoint trimming has no answer-quality test. None of these results supports a claim about the measured A, B, C, or K accuracy differences.

Study 3 has two repeats per setup and two completed pools, so its direction count is a coarse summary, not a test. Its pools were close to the ceiling. In pool 1 every setup except Split landed all 20 items, pool 2's rooms landed 19 or 20, and both pool 3 runs landed 20. Items passing therefore cannot separate Solo from the rooms or the two rooms from each other; time and cost can, and they are reported rather than tested. The ceiling gate counted items a practice Solo run passed, but a seat that stops early passes fewer items without the items being harder: in both pool 3 practice runs every attempted item passed, and the second still cleared the gate. Items were written and reviewed by AI agents. All runs shared one machine, whose test runner allowed only a few test runs at once, so larger rooms waited longer for tests. Pool 3 was stopped after two of its eight runs, at the operator's decision, once it was clear the pool could not separate the setups.
