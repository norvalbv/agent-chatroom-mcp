\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Research questions not run}

This study executed RQ1 (and its arm-K extension) only. RQ2 -- the chatroom against a no-chat
builder-plus-reviewer pair (protocol \S2.1, arm~B) -- and RQ3 -- per-mechanic ablation across the seven
hub mechanics listed in protocol \S2.3 -- are both pre-registered but have no execution behind them in
this codebase: arm~B has never been run on any task here (\texttt{paper/budget.md}\,\S5: "the two
cheapest-looking conditions are also the two with no cost evidence at all"), and none of the seven
ablations has a real-model run. Both are listed as future work, not reported as negative results.

\subsection{Task-suite retirements}

\texttt{bench-long-brief} was retired from this suite (\texttt{paper/amendments.md}, 2026-09-18): its
handoff/survival scoring is structurally unmeasurable on a single-seat arm~A, so it cannot support an
accuracy comparison across arms under this study's method. About 45 candidate task designs were piloted
in all, including the three admitted (\texttt{paper/amendments.md}: "About 45 candidate designs in all;
three are in band"); the roughly 42 that were not admitted span cross-document retrieval, data cleaning,
layered rule precedence, tick-scheduler simulation, and several invented-machine languages, and were
rejected either at ceiling (3 of 3, or above 0.9 on ten seeds) or as ambiguous. None of that
negative-result space is represented in the primary comparison; it is summarized, not reproduced, in
\texttt{paper/amendments.md}.

\subsection{Two families is not a benchmark}

The admitted suite spans two failure-mechanism families (scope-capture; exact numeric formatting), not
a broad benchmark. A mechanic, or an aggregation strategy, that helps on one family and hurts on the
other is exactly the kind of result this suite exists to surface, but three tasks in two families cannot
support a general claim about "code tasks" or "aggregation" beyond these two specific mechanisms
(Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

\subsection{System-level cost and process findings are separate contributions}

The token-consumption round (\texttt{docs/token-round-0918.md}) and the peer-review round
(\texttt{docs/review-round-0918.md}) measure this project's own build process, not the arms compared in
Section~\ref{sec:results}; they are reported as system contributions in their own right
(Section~\ref{sec:system}) with their own methodology, and are not folded into RQ1's task-success or
cost-per-correct-answer numbers.

\subsection{Future work}

Completing RQ2 and RQ3; extending the window-control design (Section~\ref{sec:results}) to
\texttt{stamp-2} and \texttt{bench-printf-format}, which the redesigned, reduced-scope control
(Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}) does not cover; a task suite broad enough to support family-level
generalization across more than two failure mechanisms; and, if the chatroom's mechanics are ever shown
to add value beyond sampling on a broader suite, testing whether a learned or structural minority-override
mechanism (arXiv:2606.29270) could recover further value specifically on tasks like
\texttt{bench-printf-format}, where the correct answer is not the population's plurality and no amount
of aggregation-by-agreement can find it.

The mid-study regime shift documented in Section~\ref{sec:threats} motivates three concrete protocol
changes for any future run on this harness, stated in \texttt{paper/amendments.md} and repeated here
because they bear directly on reproducibility: record output tokens per run as a regime indicator so a
shift is visible immediately rather than discovered after the fact; pin a dated model snapshot where the
provider allows it, rather than relying on a model alias whose behavior can change mid-session with no
corresponding version bump; and never pool runs collected across a detected regime boundary into one
estimate, even when, as here, the headline pass-rate numbers turn out not to have been affected.
