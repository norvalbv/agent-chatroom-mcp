\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Reused and extended from the pre-registration (\texttt{paper/protocol.md}\,\S8), with the arm-K-specific
threats added by the later pre-registration (\texttt{paper/prereg-arm-k.md}) and by findings made while
running it.

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Single-codebase, two-family generalization.} All three admitted tasks come from a suite
  screened on this project's own hub and its own \texttt{claude}-CLI harness; nothing here is evidence
  about other multi-agent frameworks, and the two admitted failure families -- scope-capture in an
  invented language, and exact numeric formatting against a host built-in -- do not license a claim
  about coordination or aggregation in general (\texttt{paper/amendments.md}, "Result of the second
  suite room").

\item \textbf{Admission-rate selection bias.} A task enters the primary suite because its ten pilot
  seeds landed in the pre-registered 0.3--0.7 pass-rate band; a task whose true rate is 0.85 lands there
  by chance about 18\% of the time, and admitted tasks regress toward the mean on fresh seeds.
  \texttt{bench-printf-format}'s admission record (5/15 = 0.33) is already flagged in
  \texttt{tasks/SUITE.json} as the weakest primary task for this reason; its grid-seed rate (14/40 =
  0.35) landed close to the admission rate, but this was not guaranteed in advance and should not be
  read as validating the admission rule generally.

\item \textbf{Arm A's budget is not an experimenter-chosen constant.} Protocol \S2.1 sets arm~A's
  per-run ceiling to arm~C's own realized spend on the same (task, seed); arm~A cannot be run until
  arm~C already has, a sequencing dependency a fully independent-arms design would not have (flagged
  during the protocol's own review, room \texttt{swarm-113947-ne8o-room}, challenge
  \texttt{ch\_e72f2514}).

\item \textbf{Arm K's timing window differs from arm C's.} The 40 arm-C runs analyzed here
  (\texttt{bench/results/rq1-suite}) were produced in an earlier grid than the 120 arm-K groups
  (\texttt{bench/results/rq1-arm-k}); arms A and C, and the 40 arm-A budget-matched runs, were
  interleaved per seed within the original grid, but arm~K was not interleaved with either
  (\texttt{paper/amendments.md}, 2026-09-19 thinking-budget entry). If the single-agent rate is not
  stable across the time windows the two grids were run in, the K-vs-C comparison is confounded by time,
  not only by sampling noise. A contemporaneous, interleaved arm-C/arm-K sample
  (\texttt{bench/results/rq1-window-control}) was designed specifically to test this; its status as of
  this section is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} rather than assumed complete.

\item \textbf{A confirmed, undocumented regime shift in the model behind the \texttt{sonnet} alias
  during this study.} Between approximately 15:30 and 16:45 local time on 2026-09-19, with no change to
  the harness, CLI version, project settings, or recorded model id, single-agent output-token counts on
  \texttt{stamp-interpreter} rose roughly sixfold (about 1.6K to about 10K output tokens per attempt,
  cost about \$0.058 to \$0.18--0.26) and the model's own thinking-block length grew from about 1.3K to
  about 10.5K tokens; the same shift appears in arm-C seats' per-seat output tokens and per-run cost
  (\texttt{paper/amendments.md}, 2026-09-19 thinking-budget entry). The original 40-run RQ1 grid and the
  400-attempt arm-K measurement discussed above were both run, and confirmed stable, before this shift;
  the pass-rate numbers reported in this paper are therefore not put in question by it. What the shift
  does affect: (i) every cost and token figure in this paper is regime-specific and must be read together
  with its date and its observed output tokens per run, not extrapolated to a different time; (ii) the
  window-control design (above) was originally interrupted mid-run by the cost increase this shift caused
  and was redesigned at reduced scope, entirely inside the post-shift regime, once discovered
  (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}); and (iii) no comparison in this paper should be read as valid
  outside the specific time window its own runs were collected in. We report this as a confirmed
  mechanism, not a hypothesis: it is the maintainer's own direct observation of the shift, not an
  inference from the pass-rate numbers.

\item \textbf{Provider and estimate nondeterminism, independent of the regime shift above.}
  Single-agent pass-rate estimates were noisy in both directions across independently drawn samples of
  the identical harness and prompt, all collected before the regime shift just described: for
  \texttt{stamp-interpreter}, 0.50 at $n=10$ in the admission pilot, 0.825 at $n=40$ in the RQ1 grid, and
  0.675 at $n=400$ in the arm-K attempt-level measurement. A 60-run budget-cap control
  (\texttt{bench/results/rq1-drift-control}: 23/30 at a \$0.30 per-attempt cap, 18/30 at a \$0.58
  per-attempt cap, also pre-shift) rules out the attempt-level spending cap as the cause of this
  discrepancy between samples, but the underlying source of it -- ordinary sampling noise at these
  sample sizes, or some other pre-shift source of variation -- is not identified and is not claimed to
  be; it is a separate, unresolved question from the confirmed post-shift regime change above.

\item \textbf{Selector sensitivity on the printf task.} Arm~K's printf selector (execution agreement
  over 9650 probes) is demonstrably sensitive to the probe set: the two candidate clusters in the
  40-attempt pool differ on exactly 24 of 9650 probes, all instances of the one known specification
  trap. A probe generator that omitted that one case would make the selector degenerate to "always
  submit the first attempt" (\texttt{paper/amendments.md}, printf calibration entry). The reported
  arm-K printf number is a property of this specific, frozen probe set (its hash is recorded in that
  entry) and should not be read as a general property of execution-agreement selection independent of
  probe design.

\item \textbf{Protocol-authoring confound.} The pre-registration, the task-admission rule, and the
  arm-K design were themselves produced by chatroom seats coordinating through the mechanics under
  evaluation; the authors of this study were not blind to which mechanics they expected to matter. The
  falsification criteria in both pre-registrations were stated in advance specifically to bound this
  risk, not to eliminate it.

\item \textbf{A pre-registered prediction is confirmed on one program and refuted on the other, inside
  one family.} \texttt{paper/prereg-arm-k.md}'s prediction~1 claims K vs.\ A is significant on both
  \texttt{stamp} programs; this held for \texttt{stamp-2} (Holm $p=0.0090$) but not for
  \texttt{stamp-interpreter} (Holm $p=1.000$), even though arm~C beats arm~A significantly on both
  (Section~\ref{sec:discussion-beyond-sampling}). We report this as a fact, not as evidence that
  deliberation and sampling differ from each other: inferring a K-vs-C difference from a difference
  between K's and C's own significance against A would be the difference-in-significance fallacy, and
  the direct K-vs-C test is not significant on \texttt{stamp-interpreter}. Because the two programs are
  one statistical unit by this paper's own family rule, and \texttt{stamp-interpreter}'s single-agent
  rate is independently shown to be volatile across sample sizes, this pattern sits at the edge of what
  40 paired seeds can resolve either way.

\item \textbf{Statistical-test choice matters for one reported comparison.} Applying
  \texttt{protocol.md}\,\S5's literal per-comparison rule (two-proportion $z$-test unless a 2$\times$2
  cell count is below five) to \texttt{bench-printf-format}'s A-vs-C comparison (14/40 vs.\ 6/40, all
  four cells $\geq 5$) gives $p=0.039$; Fisher's exact test on the identical counts -- the test used
  throughout for every arm-K comparison in the later pre-registration -- gives $p=0.069$. Which test is
  reported as primary for this comparison, and why, is stated explicitly in Section~\ref{sec:results};
  we surface the discrepancy here rather than silently reporting whichever number is more favorable to
  either arm.

\end{itemize}
