# Swarm

One command runs the whole thing. Three shapes:

- **Planned** (what runs when you pass neither `--flat` nor `--groups`): a planner agent splits the task into sub-questions, worker agents investigate each in their own room, each room's lead carries its conclusion to a leads room, and a verifier with a unanimous-quorum vote checks the claims against the real project. Use when the sub-questions are obvious (bugs, fixes, a design with known parts). The records hold 5 planned runs, each about 40 minutes or less, none since 11 Oct: for anything longer pass `--flat`.
- **Flat** (`--flat`): no planner. Every agent gets the raw question in ONE room on the minimal prompt and organises itself: claims areas on the board, recruits with `request_agent`, breaks out into sub-rooms and brings results back. The verifier sits in the same room. Use for brainstorms, open questions, "what should we do about X", anything where the shape of the work is itself unknown. Benji prefers this when the task is loose. At launch each worker already starts on a different item of the brief's first numbered list (`src/seat-targets.ts`, off with `--no-seat-targets`). So when the work has parts, number them, and put that list before any numbered rules: in swarm-005940-77tu the rules came first and the seats opened on "rule 2".
- **Groups** (`--groups groups.json [--digest-every <min>]`): you write the groups yourself, each with its own room and its own brief (for example one builds a case and one tries to break it), and they talk only within their room. A fresh agent carries findings between the rooms on a timer, and a referee outside every group holds the scoring rule and checks results. This is the Navier-Stokes shape; details are under "Groups" below.

Choosing between flat and groups, from the records (swarm-005940-77tu `evidence/room-shape`: 169 runs, 151 flat of which 13 opened break-outs, 8 grouped of which the operator stopped 4, 5 planned, and 5 of unknown shape or stopped at once; sources arXiv:2512.08296 and 2502.02533):
- Shape changed the headline answer in none of the 4 families that asked one question in both shapes: Polymarket research (flat 5y46 against grouped mrw4 and 54vu), the PR-pipeline decision (jete against 8i07), the devkit benchmark (jn9l and qv0j against 1nwp), and the tky5 fixtures (2 x 2, both always correct).
- Grouped cost 1.1 to 6x as much and concluded later (jete $22 at 9 min, 8i07 $99 at 99 min). First evidence came at about the same time: 0-1 min flat, 4-8 min grouped. Cost follows seats x hours. Grouped runs had 2 to 6x the seats, and per-request context was about 150k in both shapes on this hub.
- Grouped's one advantage: every long grouped run the operator did not stop concluded (4 of 4), because the leads merge forces it. Long flat runs concluded 38 of 49 times. The one flat run on the same task (5y46, 7 h, on an older hub) never concluded, though its findings pointed to the same answer.
- The long builds on one codebase ran flat (14 runs and every RSI round), with break-outs for the parts. The exception is 1nwp, which built three separate benchmarks, one per group.
- Confounds: no family is the same brief on the same day over hours. Date, hub version, seat count and operator all differ, and answer quality beyond the headline was not scored. The literature points the same way: shape should follow the task's structure, and each coordination layer costs turns. A leads room over groups is 2512.08296's "hybrid", which has the lowest success per token.

| Groups are likely better when | Flat is likely better when |
|---|---|
| a claim needs a real attack: one team builds it, another tries to break it, and neither should share the other's framing | everyone works on one codebase and merges to one branch (separate rooms lose work to merge conflicts; that is the measured reason one room beat split agents on 20-item backlogs) |
| a single room tends to settle on one answer early and different approaches need to stay alive | the work needs lots of quick back-and-forth between agents |
| the work splits into separate approaches whose findings help each other now and then | cost and time matter more than independence |
| (arXiv 2409.14051: at six agents, two groups of three with a digest beat one room, on short closed-answer tasks only) | |

Between these two, pick flat unless the user asks for groups or the task clearly fits the left column. If a long flat room must end with an answer, say in the brief when to propose; groups get that from the leads merge. Seats are not put into groups automatically at launch, because no record shows grouping paying for its cost (`decision/room-shape`). The automatic parts are the seat targets above and break-outs that seats open when a part separates. After a run, `scripts/runs-table.ts --by-run` prints its shape, with each break-out's minute and who opened it. That table counts a group lead's usage once. A same-brief comparison of grouped against flat over hours is still the open experiment.

## Run it

Always run the orchestrator. Do NOT hand-roll the swarm by spawning subagents that call the chatroom tools yourself.

```bash
cd "$HOME/Desktop/Personal and learning/agent-chatroom-mcp"   # same folder under the home directory on both machines
node dist/swarm.js "<task in the user's words>" --agents <N> --cwd "<absolute project dir>" \
  [--flat | --groups groups.json [--digest-every <min>]] [--resources resources.md] [--preflight "<cmd>" | --preflight-strict "<cmd>"] [--models sonnet,haiku,fable,opus] [--lead-model opus] [--verifier-model fable] \
  [--apply | --full-access] [--codex <k>] [--openrouter <k>] [--openrouter-reasoning low|medium|high] [--sandbox] [--fresh-challenger] [--timeout <minutes>] [--done-when "<criterion>"] [--verify "<what the verifier checks>"]
```

- `--agents N`: total including the verifier (default 4). "a few" = 4; for "lots", start at 5 to 6 and let the room recruit into named items (see Sizing a run). Above 6 in one room the launcher prints a size note, which is not a refusal. **Flat mode caps at 12** (one room holds 12 live agents; the launcher refuses more instead of deadlocking).
- `--models a,b,c`: Claude model mix rotated over workers. Aliases that work: `haiku`, `sonnet`, `opus`, `fable`. `--lead-model` (planned only), `--verifier-model`, `--planner-model`. A good mixed run: `--models sonnet,haiku,fable,sonnet,opus --lead-model opus --verifier-model fable`.
- `--codex k`: k workers on OpenAI Codex, rotating over `--codex-models`. Only when the user asks and has quota. Launcher and recruits use the native `codex app-server` adapter: prompts travel on stdin, the thread uses read-only permissions unless `--full-access` provides a worktree, and chatroom tools are approved explicitly. Write seats retain their configured sandbox. Pending addressed messages are injected through `turn/steer` with the active turn ID; successful acceptance acknowledges the hub, while rejected or ambiguous delivery remains available through normal hub tools. Runtime polling does not consume a model turn. `<name>.events.jsonl` preserves normalized tool events and `<name>.usage.json` uses cumulative thread usage (USD remains unknown).
- `--openrouter k`: k workers on OpenRouter models, rotating over `--openrouter-models` (default `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash,google/gemini-3.8-flash,z-ai/glm-5.3`; any slug from openrouter.ai/models works). Needs `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` exported — the launcher refuses to start without it rather than leaving dead seats in the quorum. Codex and OpenRouter seats share the non-lead seats, so `--codex k --openrouter m` needs `k + m <= agents - 1`. A seat is `src/seat.ts` (provider-independent loop: full hub tool descriptions and the hub's instructions in the system prompt, the hub's `hint` lifted into a user turn when it is about this seat, reasoning blocks passed back, a wall-clock budget equal to `--timeout` after which it leaves the room) plus `src/openrouter.ts` (the provider and CLI). Local tools: `read_file` / `list_dir` / `search` / `web_fetch` / `run_command`, read-only unless `--full-access` (then each seat gets its own worktree and branch with `node_modules` linked, so `npm run build` and the tests work there). Pattern kills (`pkill`, `killall`) are refused in every seat; a brief that has agents start test hubs must tell them to stop them by pid (`kill $(lsof -ti:PORT)`), and never say "kill your hub" without that, because a Claude verifier with Bash has no such guard. **Before trusting a new model in a swarm, trial it**: `OPENROUTER_API_KEY=… npm run seat:trial -- --model <slug>` runs one seat against a throwaway hub and prints whether it joins, opens, replies when @-addressed, engages with a proposal and leaves, with timings and cost (log in `logs/`).
- **Proactive handoff** (OpenRouter/`src/seat.ts` seats, `docs/decisions/r1-proactive-handoff.md`): a seat still holding a `claim/*` writes `handoff/<area>` and leaves cleanly before its step cap, a token budget, the context ceiling, or (`--handoff-idle-turns`, default 20) too many consecutive turns producing no actionable hub hint and no outbound `send_message`/`board_set` — instead of dying mid-work or trimming its own claim out of context. Flags: `--handoff-step-max`, `--handoff-step-fraction` (default 0.75), `--handoff-prompt-tokens` (default 6,000,000), `--handoff-context-fraction` (default 0.9), `--handoff-idle-turns`, `--no-handoff` to opt out. Verified offline, no network: `scripts/handoff-regression.ts`.
- `--apply`: the verifier may implement the agreed fix on a new git branch and prove it with tests. `--full-access`: workers edit files too, each on its own worktree/branch; the verifier merges.
- `--fresh-challenger` (off by default): a hub the launcher starts gets `CHATROOM_FRESH_CHALLENGER=1` (a hub started by hand needs that variable itself). When a proposal opens, the hub recruits one read-only seat that has not seen the discussion, briefed with the proposal text only, to check one claim against the repository and file it as a challenge with `command` if it fails. The conclusion waits for that seat until it challenges, leaves, or `CHATROOM_FRESH_CHALLENGER_WAIT_MIN` (default 6) passes. One per proposal, at most two per room, on the hub's recruit policy model. Evidence and the two rooms watched: `docs/experiments/2026-10-10-fresh-challenger.md`.
- `--sandbox` (off by default; turning it on by default is the owner's call): every seat's shell runs in an OS sandbox, so a seat's `pkill -f <word>` or `killall` cannot reach the hub, the launcher or another seat (the pool run where one seat's pkill killed 14 of 15). Claude seats get Claude Code's own sandbox in `--settings` (strict, fails closed, npm/GitHub/hub allowlist, local binding; a read-only seat's working directory is denied); OpenRouter seats wrap `run_command` in `@anthropic-ai/sandbox-runtime`; a hub the launcher starts gets `CHATROOM_SANDBOX=1` so recruits match (a hub started by hand needs that variable itself). Codex seats are not covered. Each command is its own sandbox, so a process a seat starts cannot be stopped from a later command: sandboxed seats on this hub are told to start and stop a private hub (`node dist/index.js`) inside one command, and they cannot launch agents from inside the sandbox. `src/sandbox.ts` lists what the probes found.
- `--no-web`: no Claude seat gets `WebSearch` or `WebFetch` (dropped from every seat's allowed tools), for benchmark tasks whose answers are published online; the Terminal-Bench room adapter passes it so a room gets the same tools as Harbor's solo agent run with those two disallowed.
- `--no-carry`: seats start from the brief alone. No settled decision axes or PRIOR RUNS list is put in front of the prompt, and Claude Code's auto-memory is off for launcher seats (hub recruits are unaffected). Use it when independent starts matter; shared carried-forward context makes same-model seats open alike.
- `--compact-at <tokens>`: every Claude seat compacts its transcript at this size (100000 to 1000000, a plain integer), and so do the recruits of a hub this launcher starts. A seat re-reads its transcript on every request; unset, Claude Code compacts only near the model's limit, and in an eight-hour room that was two thirds of the bill. The board, not the transcript, is what survives a compaction: use it for long rooms and say so in the brief. Use 200000 or more: at 100000 a seat reading large files stopped with "Autocompact is thrashing".
- When the project is this hub, `--full-access` seats and edit-rights recruits are also told to run their change, not only its tests: `npm run build`, then `CHATROOM_INSECURE_LOCAL=1 node dist/swarm.js "<brief>" --flat --agents 3 --models claude-opus-5-5 --verifier-model claude-opus-5-5 --timeout 10 --port <free port>` from their worktree starts a private hub from their build with agents on it (stop it by PID afterwards). They name the dev room they watched when asking for review (`devHubRule`, src/env.ts). Without `CHATROOM_INSECURE_LOCAL=1`, which seats have stripped, that private hub refuses the launcher's room create with a 403 and the room silently runs on default policy.
- `--named`: real names inside worker rooms (default pseudonyms). Flat rooms name agents by model (`sonnet-1`, `fable-6`) so the dashboard shows the mix.
- `--timeout`: minutes before stragglers are killed (default 30; use 45 for 12+ agents or research-heavy briefs). With `--full-access`, anything still running inside this run's worktrees (`.swarm-worktrees/<run>/`, such as a dev hub a seat detached) is stopped as well, at the timeout, on Ctrl-C/SIGTERM and when the run ends; the project checkout itself is never swept. Do not keep a shell open in a seat's worktree while its run is live.
- `--require-verification`: the launcher creates the room with `require_verification` before any seat joins (via `POST /rooms/:room/create`), so no proposal can pass without a `verify/*` board entry by someone other than its author whose first line is JSON `{"proposal":"<id>","command":"<the check you ran>","cwd":"<working dir>","base_commit":"<parent commit, before the change>","base_exit_code":1,"commit":"<the proposal's commit>","exit_code":0,"output_tail":"<last lines of real output at commit>"}` naming that proposal: fail-to-pass in SWE-bench's sense, one check that failed at the parent commit (`base_exit_code` nonzero) and passes at the proposal's (`exit_code` 0). A refactor with no behaviour change passes at both and adds `"refactor":true`; the conclusion notice names which path passed it. Optional `"kind"`: `existing_tests`, `own_check` or `exercised`; prose may follow. An entry without that head does not count, and `blocked_by` names up to the two latest entries about the proposal that do not count, and what each lacks. Reviewers exercise the change the way its users would rather than only rerunning the author's tests, and write what they could not verify under the head (nothing verified: `verify/<area>.partial`, which never counts). The head is self-reported: the hub runs nothing (docs/decisions/proposed/verify-head-fail-to-pass.md); it only looks up `commit` and `base_commit` in the writer's own worktree and says in the board notice when one does not exist there. A per-commit review written before any proposal exists goes under `review/<short-sha>` with the same head and `"proposal":"pending"`: it is author-only like `verify/*`, covers that commit in the final proposal's `unverified_commits` and `verification_gaps`, and never opens the gate; `room_status.reviews` lists, per commit, the entries that name it and whether each passes (`scripts/review-links-regression.ts`). Use it for build rooms: "done" is then hub-enforced, not a vote.
- **Reviewer assignment**: creating `claim/<area>` assigns a reviewer, not a volunteer — the hub picks the least-recently-verifying active participant who is not the owner and not a second identity on the owner's own MCP session (never-verified sorts first, ties break by earliest join), fixed at creation and unchanged by later edits to the same claim. Both the claimant and the reviewer are told; the reviewer's notice is a hub-authored `@name` chat line (not a system notice — chat-kind is what makes the wake survive the read cursor already having moved past it) that wakes a held `wait_for_messages(hold_until_actionable=true)`. While that reviewer is active, `require_verification` accepts only their `verify/*` entry naming the proposal; once they leave, it falls back to any non-author entry, so nobody is stuck waiting on someone who is gone. See the claim's `reviewer` field in `room_status`/`board_get` and the dashboard's People ("reviewing: …") and Board tabs.
- **Baseline-freeze guard** (`.husky/pre-commit`, `scripts/guard-baseline-freeze.mjs`): a commit touching `.devkit/baselines/**` or `.devkit/config.json` is refused unless a `verify/baseline-<area>` board entry by a different seat names the staged tree (`git write-tree`) — an author cannot bank their own baseline change unreviewed (e.g. commit 15d3717, which raised the fan-out cap in the same commit as the growth it grandfathered). Fails CLOSED if the hub is unreachable. The maintainer's override (`CHATROOM_BASELINE_FREEZE_OVERRIDE=1 git commit ...`) is excluded from every seat's environment (`SEAT_ENV_EXCLUSIONS`, `src/env.ts`) — do not try to set it from a seat.
- `--verifier-openrouter <slug>`: the verifier seat on an OpenRouter model (a run on a free model then costs nothing).
- **Self-improvement lobby** (`--flat --agents 40 --openrouter 39 --verifier-openrouter <slug> --prompt loop.md --quorum supermajority --require-verification --respawn --timeout 240`): `--quorum supermajority` (ceil(0.75 x electorate)) is now the recommended default for flat runs that used to pass plain `majority`; `unanimous` and `majority` are still available (`--quorum unanimous|majority|supermajority`). One large room on `prompts/loop.md` (quiet messages for working exchanges, fetched-not-remembered research, break-out rooms for depth and for building with worktrees per write-enabled recruit, hub-enforced verify entries). Needs the hub started with `CHATROOM_MAX_LIVE_PER_ROOM=80 CHATROOM_MAX_LIVE_AGENTS=400 CHATROOM_MAX_RECRUITS_PER_ROOM=400 CHATROOM_MAX_RECRUITS_PER_RUN=1000 CHATROOM_MAX_RECRUIT_DEPTH=4 CHATROOM_MAX_ROOMS_PER_RUN=100`. Seats absorb three empty waits per model turn (`--idle-waits`), which is what fits 40 seats under a 100 req/min model limit; `--respawn` relaunches a seat that exits while its room is open.
- A repeat `join_room` from the same session and name is idempotent, and the seat refuses to join a room it is already in (under any name) and reminds the model of its identity every turn; a duplicate persona like `bench-3b` beside `bench-3` was a context drop, not a second agent (`scripts/session-identity.test.ts`).
- A seat heads-down in local work shows as `working: <tool> · <age>` in the People tab (seat heartbeats over HTTP each step); `last_seen_at` in `GET /rooms/<room>` is the latest of hub call and heartbeat. Judge liveness by that, not by message count. After a hub restart every seat is restored as left until its next hub call; one whose process still heartbeats (by seat key), or that was in the room at the restart and has been silent under 10 min since, shows `liveness.status: "away"` in `room_status`, and `request_agent(replacing=)` refuses it with that evidence, so a quiet live seat is not duplicated (`scripts/away-seat-regression.ts`). The other way round, a killed CLI never sends an MCP DELETE and used to read as connected for 30 min, so `replace_participant` refused a dead seat; now the launcher (swarm.js and recruits) posts an exit receipt on child close (`POST /heartbeat {seat_key, exited:true}`), the hub closes every session that key opened, the seat leaves with "seat process exited" and can be replaced at once (`scripts/seat-exit-regression.ts`); click the person to see their last 60 steps with the actual commands (`GET /rooms/<room>/participants/<name>/activity`). The same tool hook steers a busy `claude -p` seat: it runs on PreToolUse and PostToolUse, injects any @-mention or reply addressed to the seat as hook `additionalContext` at the next local tool boundary, and acks it (`POST /steer/ack`), so the seat answers mid-job without a wait (busy-seat bench: ~51 s → ~7 s, docs/experiments/2026-10-03-steering-claude-seats.md).
- **A second attack, review or verify entry**: `attack/<key>`, `review/<sha>` and `verify/<area>` stay author-only, but a second seat writing one that another seat already holds is no longer refused after the work: the hub keeps it beside the first as `<key>.<writer>` and says so in the reply (`asked_key`, `landed_hint`) and in the board notice. A second attack is an open attack on the same entry and its author is asked to answer it; `room_status.reviews` lists both reviews of a commit. Clearing a peer's entry, or replacing it with `overwrite=true`, is still refused (`scripts/second-scrutiny-regression.ts`).
- **Checks are linked to what they check, and say what they found**: an entry under `rerun/`, `audit/`, `review/`, `valid/`, `critique/`, `check/`, `attack/` or `verify/` (a `review/` or `verify/` entry with a verify head excepted: that is a review of the commit its head names) is resolved by `src/hub/checks.ts` to the entries by other seats it checks: by its key (the rest of the key is the entry's key, also with its slashes as dashes), by an entry's id (`audit/Q4-partA` for `exp/Q4`, `rerun/T-B3-2-by-<seat>` for `result/T-B3-2-...`; when several entries share the id, the one the check's text names), or by naming a key as a whole key in its text. Rooms keep their own vocabulary; no key rule is imposed. The entry keeps `checks`, the room log gets a `check` event `{key, by, of, how, verdict}`, and the `board_set` reply names the links and, when the first line does not declare one, asks for `VERDICT: holds|fails|partial — <clause>` (only that line is read: prose verdicts were wrong too often to count, decision/check-verdict-not-built). `runs-table` uses the same resolver: its reviewed column counts checks of `result/*` by key shape or text, "entries checked" covers rooms with no `result/*` keys, the reviewed results are split by declared verdict, and `--by-run` counts results checked from another room of the same run (`scripts/check-links-regression.ts`, `check-verdict-regression.ts`, `cross-room-checks-regression.ts`).
- **Size limits are stated**: the room summary `join_room` returns carries `limits` `{message_chars, board_entry_chars, opening_chars}`; a message over the limit is told its size and to `board_set` the text and send a one-line pointer; a board entry or note over 8,000 characters is told its size and how to split or append (`scripts/limits-stated-regression.ts`).
- **The hub's clock**: every `wait_for_messages`, `send_message` and `board_set` reply carries `hub_time` (UTC to the minute), so a brief no longer has to say "check the clock with `date -u`". When a message or board entry states a present-tense time ("as of 14:20 UTC", "Started 14:50 UTC", "written 12:35Z") more than 5 minutes from the hub's clock, the writer's reply carries a `time_note` with the real time and the room log gets a `time_claim` event `{by, where, stated, delta_min}`; schedules ("at 17:00 UTC") and times quoted from logs are left alone (`src/hub/clock.ts`, `scripts/hub-clock-regression.ts`).
- `leave_room` needs a `reason` (what was finished, where it is on the board, what is undone); it is posted in the room and shown in the dashboard's People tab, so a departure can be read and, if others disagree, answered with a recruit. While the room is open, a leave that abandons a `claim/*` without a `handoff/*` or an unanswered @-ask is refused once with what to write first (`scripts/leave-regression.ts`). Once the room has concluded or closed, `leave_room` never refuses for either reason (a reason is still required): the hub announces every `claim/*` released in one system line, without deleting them (a seat's note is sometimes the only record of what it did), and coordination writes (`claim/`, `take/`, `hold/`, `draft/`, `idea/`) are refused from then on, with a plain message. For `CHATROOM_LATE_WRITE_MS` (default 3 hours, 0 = off) after the conclusion the board still takes a late result, `verify/*`, `review/*` or `handoff/*`: the entry is marked `late` in `room_status` and in its board notice, logged as a `late_write` event (with `contradicts` and one room line when it is a verdict failing a commit the conclusion cites), and never changes the conclusion; a breakout that concludes after its parent delivers its conclusion the same way, as a late `inbox/<breakout>/conclusion` (`scripts/late-writes-regression.ts`); after the window, and in a closed room, every write is refused. `verify/*`, `handoff/*` and `claim/*` entries all stay readable with `board_get` (`scripts/leave-post-conclusion-regression.ts`).
- `--respawn`: a seat that exits while its room is open is relaunched as `<name>-r1..r3` only when the room still needs it: non-zero exit, a `claim/*` of theirs with no `handoff/*`, the verifier, or fewer active seats than the floor, which is 3 between proposals and, while a proposal is open, max(3, ceil(expected/2)) for majority, max(3, ceil(expected*0.75)) for supermajority, or expected for unanimous. A seat that finished, handed over and left is not replaced; the launcher logs the reason either way (`src/respawn.ts`, `scripts/respawn-regression.ts`). Two runs of 20 with unconditional respawn churned 70 replacements in 30 minutes. A departed seat's `claim/*` stays owned so respawn sees it, but its registered successor can take it over at once and any seat can after 10 minutes (`CHATROOM_STALE_CLAIM_MS`); each takeover posts a system line, and `room_status` lists `stale_claims`.
- `--seat-memory-mb <n>`: a budget on what the processes one seat starts may hold together (its own agent process is not counted). The hub samples every seat's processes by seat key every 15 s (`room_status` shows `memory` per seat; `GET /memory` lists tagged processes of seats that have left, which are never stopped); past the budget the seat is told the pid and the number, and at the next check its largest process is stopped by PID and it is told what to change. Seats read the figure as `CHATROOM_SEAT_MEMORY_MB`. It is a promise per seat, not a machine cap: each recruit and consolidator seat has its own key and its own budget, so size it as (RAM minus about 8 GB) over the most seats that will run at once, recruits included, e.g. 2500 for six on 24 GB. Without the flag the hub only measures.
- `take/<what>` on the board: a small take (an audit, a rerun, a turn on a shared resource), one plain line, no reviewer. A line starting with `@name` gives it to that seat, who takes it by writing the key or declines by clearing it; a write to a take someone else holds is refused with the holder. `room_status` lists each seat's `holds` and `offered`, and the room log has one `take` record per step.
- **Fleet** (`node dist/fleet.js fleet/<spec>.json --model <slug> [--only a,b] [--consolidate --consolidate-from <summary.md,...>] [--require-verification] [--full-access]`): one flat run per area from a JSON spec (areas with `lenses` become competing rooms), staggered, with every conclusion gathered into `swarms/fleet-<id>/summary.md`; `--consolidate` runs one more room that merges the summaries into one ranked consensus. Rate limits are per model and account: `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731` allows 100 requests/min, which fits about 36 seats (three rooms); `fleet/run-batches.sh` runs the rest back to back.

Every prompt automatically carries the project's settled axes (`docs/decisions/`), the sources already read, and the last six prior runs (`swarms/*/report.md`), so tell agents to ratify or refute by reference rather than re-derive.

## Groups: the Navier-Stokes shape (`--groups`, `--digest-every`)

Separate groups that talk only within their own room, with opposing or different briefs, and a fresh agent that carries the useful findings between them on a timer (OpenAI's Navier-Stokes effort, scaled down; sources and what carries over at six seats: `docs/self-improvement-swarm-003601-tky5.md`, `sources/group-shape-summary`). Use it when the user asks for opposing teams, build-versus-break, or that shape by name. Otherwise the flat room is the default: in the records, groups cost 1.1 to 6x as much as flat and changed no headline answer (see the start of this skill).

```bash
node dist/swarm.js "<task, with the scoring rule both groups share>" --groups groups.json --digest-every 30 \
  --models claude-opus-5-5 --lead-model claude-opus-5-5 --verifier-model claude-opus-5-5 \
  --full-access --respawn --no-carry --quorum supermajority --timeout 480 --cwd "<project>" [--resources resources.md]
```

- `--groups <file.json>` replaces the planner and cannot be combined with `--flat`: `{"done_when": "...", "verifier_directive": "...", "groups": [{"id": "build", "title": "Build the case", "workers": 3, "brief": "..."}, {"id": "break", ...}]}`. Each group gets its own room `<run>-<id>` and its own brief on `prompts/group.md`; `workers` is at least 2 (a room of one cannot conclude); `--agents` is ignored (the total is the groups' workers plus the verifier). The verifier is the referee in `<run>-leads`, with the file's `verifier_directive`. When a group concludes, its first seat (the lead, on `--lead-model`) carries the group's conclusion to the leads room (`prompts/lead-tail.md`), where one final proposal is adopted. Do not use `leads` as a group id (it collides with that room). Group ids are lower-case letters, digits and hyphens.
- Each group keeps its current answer on its board as `position`. Isolation is a sentence in the brief, not enforced: any seat can read another room over HTTP. Reads of a room by a seat outside it are counted in `room_status` (`outside_calls`), a lower bound.
- `--digest-every <min>` (needs `--groups`): each pass is a fresh consolidator seat (`prompts/consolidator.md`), never two at once. It joins the leads room and reads each group's room over HTTP without joining it. Each group that has a `position` gets one note by `post_to_room` (key `digest`, `ack_required`, so the group must acknowledge it before it can propose; the same key each pass, so only the latest is held). The pass writes `digest/<n>` on the leads board as the next pass's cursor. A group with no `position` yet is sent nothing. No pass runs once every group room has concluded. Passes and acknowledgements are in `result.json` (`digests`, `digestAcks`) and `report.md`, and the report shows each group's position even if it never concluded.
- A room holds at most 10 notes awaiting acknowledgement, counting only `ack_required` ones (rulings and FYIs sent without it never fill it); a note past that is refused, the receiving room is told which notes to acknowledge, and its log gets an `inbox_refused` event. Any seat may acknowledge: a second seat's reply is added under the first. After a grouped run, `npx tsx scripts/inbox-table.ts data/<run>-*.jsonl` gives per room the notes received, acknowledgement latency, bare acks against replies, refusals and how long the inbox was full (baseline before this rule: 0btz-build full for 49 min, 1nwp-external for 28).
- A concluded room refuses notes and proposals, so a group must not conclude before the last round: brief the groups to propose late (the Polymarket kit: groups propose from T-90 and conclude by T-60; the last hour is the leads-room merge).
- Give both groups the same fixed scoring rule in the task before they split: two agents briefed with opposite goals on one dataset each found what they were told to find (2604.22080).
- Measured so far, over 4 families of runs that asked one question in both shapes (start of this skill): grouped and flat reached the same headline answer every time; grouped cost 1.1 to 6x and concluded later, but it always concluded. Whether groups give better answers over hours is unmeasured: no same-brief run has compared them.
- `--resources <file.md>`: every `## <name>` section goes on every room's board as `resource/<name>` before a seat starts, and the hub names the `resource/` prefix to each seat on join. It works with any shape and survives `--no-carry`. Use it for data, tools and limits already on the machine. Seats used what a brief named within seconds and never looked for what it did not, and a chat line does not cross a group wall.
- **Prior art first.** Seats look for existing code, packages and papers only when the brief asks. Over 77 rooms with a work ledger, 14 of the 17 whose brief asked searched within 10 minutes (median 1.1 min), against 5 of the 60 whose brief did not, and no room ran outside code. Task type is confounded: the asking briefs were mostly hub self-improvement, the silent ones mostly trading and build rooms (evidence/research-depth, swarm-005940-77tu). In swarm-164210-9f66 the first search came at 23.5 min, after the owner had handed in two repos. For a task others have probably built before: (a) spend about 5 minutes before launch on GitHub, PyPI/npm and arXiv and put what you find in `--resources` (kvkj-b's brief opened with such a check, and its room cloned the named repo at 1.2 min; n=1); (b) make "sweep prior art in the first 15 minutes" a brief item with an owner, writing `sources/<ref>` per source and one triage line per repo (what it is, how close, whether it runs); (c) say how outside code may be run, or seats will only read it: a scratch dir, no keys or wallet, paper mode, about 10 minutes per repo, and `blocked: <reason>` counts as a result. In 9f66 the owner allowed runs offline or in paper mode, the verifier and the scout's brief narrowed that to read-only, and nothing was run; a seat that thinks an owner's rule unsafe files `needs/<name>` instead of narrowing it. (d) Afterwards the run's metrics (`report.md`, or `npx tsx scripts/run-metrics.ts data/<room>.jsonl`) carry a `prior art:` sentence: minutes to the first search of a code, package or paper site (a web search counts only when it names one), the first fetch from a code host and the first outside code run, and how many outside references were handed in against found by seats.
- `--preflight "<cmd>"` / `--preflight-strict "<cmd>"`: the launcher runs the kit's environment checks before any hub or seat starts. The command prints one JSON line per check, `{"check","status":"ok|warn|fail","value","why"?,"fix"?}`; a command that crashes, times out or prints no check counts as a FAIL. Each FAIL/WARN line is printed at launch with its owner fix, goes on every room's board as `resource/preflight-environment-at-launch`, opens every seat's brief with an instruction to file `needs/<name>` rather than route around it, and is recorded in `result.json` and `report.md`. Strict exits 2 on any FAIL. A kit whose experiment depends on the machine or its network (latency, geoblock, clock) declares one; live money uses strict. Why: the 13 Oct room measured the VPN link as unfit, the fact was trimmed out of the 22 Oct live room's resources, and that room traded from it (swarm-073112-zcd7 evidence/earlier-rooms). Test: `scripts/preflight-regression.ts`.
- A ready example: `/Users/benji/.frink/builds/room-rsi/kit/polymarket/` (`groups.json`, `task.txt`, `hub.sh` for its own supervised hub, `launch.sh`, `README.md`).

## How a room closes (since swarm-180216-182a)

- **Last call.** A proposal that has the votes while an electorate seat has not voted on its *current* version, an active seat holds a `take/*`, or a breakout of the room is still open, does not conclude at once: the hub posts `LAST CALL` naming who and what it waits for and when it closes, those seats owe a vote (a held wait wakes), and it concludes when they have all voted or the window ends (`CHATROOM_LAST_CALL_MS`, 180 s on a served hub; 0 turns it off). A room where everyone has voted closes as before. A seat whose work needs longer writes `hold/<room>`; a hold counts only while its author is in the room.
- **The verifier's agree is needed.** With a live seat of role verifier, a proposal passes only on its agree to the current version: its disagree blocks, an agree on an older version is outdated. A verifier silent for 10 minutes (no hub call, no heartbeat, no job of its own running) stops holding, and the conclusion then says `Concluded WITHOUT the verifier's agree`. Measured on one brief, 6 rooms per arm: the verifier had agreed to the concluded text in 2 of 6 rooms before, 6 of 6 after. A disagree that carries `quote` (the clause it objects to or waits on) stands across amends that keep that clause, while its author is in the room, and a verifier whose disagree stands owes no vote until the clause changes; one with no quote resets on any amend. So a verifier that has checked the text and is only waiting on an input disagrees once, quoting the pending line, rather than once per version (9f66 re-cast its HOLD five times), and writes `hold/<room>` as well when the wait may outlast its 10 silent minutes (`scripts/disagree-carry-regression.ts`).
- **Groups and breakouts.** `--groups` rooms are breakouts of the leads room (when the launcher creates the rooms: with `--quorum`, `--require-verification` or `--chair`): a group's conclusion lands on the leads board, the leads room's last call waits for open groups and tells them, and a group still open when the leads room concludes is told its answer is fixed. A break-out usually has no verifier of its own (9f66's five had none, and bk2's recruits reviewed each other), so the parent's verifier checks what its `inbox/<child>/conclusion` rests on before that line enters the parent's final; the reviewer of a commit made there is never its author.
- **What happens to a breakout** (server-chair note 6: the owner had to ask). A breakout is not stopped when its parent concludes, and nothing is deleted. In order:
  - while both are open, the parent's `room_status.breakouts` lists it, and a quiet breakout tells its parent;
  - when it concludes, its conclusion is copied onto the parent's board as `inbox/<breakout>/conclusion`. That key is the hub's: a `post_to_room` from the breakout to its parent under key `conclusion` is refused;
  - when the parent gets the votes while a breakout with seats is still open, the parent's last call waits up to its window for that breakout;
  - when the parent concludes first, the breakout is told its answer is fixed. Its own conclusion still lands there, marked late, for `CHATROOM_LATE_WRITE_MS` (3 h); after that it stays in the breakout only, and the breakout is told so;
  - closing a parent closes its open breakouts with it, recursively, each with a reason naming the parent. A concluded breakout keeps its conclusion. The archive-dead sweep skips a parent whose breakout still has seats, and archiving one by hand is refused until that breakout is done or closed;
  - after the run, every room stays listed until someone archives it. A seat that never left is reaped after 30 idle minutes.

  `room_status` and the dashboard carry the same one-line `breakout_fate` for each breakout: where its conclusion goes and until when. In the dashboard header it is a "↳ breakout of <parent>" chip; a parent shows "⤷ <breakout> · <state>" chips, and the rail groups a breakout under its parent's run even when its name lacks the run prefix. The launcher finds breakouts by the parent link as well as by name, so an unprefixed `join_room(parent=)` breakout is in `result.json` and `report.md` (src/launch/run-rooms.ts). The lifecycle code is src/hub/breakouts/lifecycle.ts.

  The record: every copy attempt is a `breakout_carry` event in the breakout's log (`delivered`, `late` or `dropped` with its reason), and each close of a parent writes `parent_closed`.

  Across 36 breakouts in 332 room logs (MAIN/data plus the 9f66 server copy):
  - 20 concluded before their parent, none after;
  - 15 belonged to 3 runs the owner stopped. In every one the chair closed the parent and all its breakouts by hand within the same second;
  - 1 unprefixed breakout (kcj5's `swarm-005617-verdict-telemetry`) is missing from its run's `result.json`.

  In 9f66, all 5 breakouts reached the parent's inbox on time. bk2 concluded 0.14 s before the parent, which had held its last call for it. The recruits left 0.2-2.7 min after their breakout concluded and spent $3.29 in total after that. The late-copy and dropped paths have not occurred in these records (`scripts/breakout-fate-regression.ts`, evidence/breakout-fate).
- **What the record says.** `conclusion.closing` carries who had not voted on the final version, the verifier's vote, the last call's length, takes still held, groups still open, and agrees from seats never sent the final text (`agreed_unsent`); votes and challenges carry `seen_s`. `scripts/runs-table.ts` shows it in the "at the close" column, for older logs too.
- **Recruits.** A write recruit's branch is cut from the HEAD of its recruiter's worktree (a launch worktree ahead of the main checkout no longer hands recruits a stale tree); a read-only recruit reads a detached snapshot of that HEAD. Commit what a recruit must build on first: uncommitted changes are not in it. When an `idea/<slug>` has had no live claim for `CHATROOM_HANDS_AFTER_MS` (10 min; 0 = off) while every active worker holds one (a recruit counts when it is on its claim's JSON `team`), the hub asks the idea's author once (the chair if the author left), as an owed ask that reaches a busy seat mid-turn: `request_agent(area="<slug>")`, `claim/<slug>` if you already build it or will after your current work, or "dropped:" and the reason. Before it, idle-wait hints named that case 9 times in 332 logs and no recruit followed; replayed over the 22 logs with ideas, it would have fired 42 times in 11 rooms, and 32 of those ideas were never resolved. In a 2-seat probe the busy author claimed the idea 7 s after the ask and replied with why it did not recruit (n=1; probe/hands-3) (evidence/hands-replay, `scripts/hands-replay.ts`, `scripts/hands-ask-regression.ts`; `hands_asked`/`hands_resolved` events, run-metrics "Ideas nobody was free to take").
- **When to recruit, take it yourself, or break out.** Take an item yourself when you are free, it is a few steps, or it follows on state you hold: a busy seat's first output came in a median 5.1 min against a recruit's 5.4 (n=125/145). Recruit one seat with `area=<slug>` when the item is self-contained with one output AND no seat is free and the room waits on it, or it needs a context that has not seen the discussion (an independent check), or its working set is bulky (sweeps, logs, many sources: a recruit's context grew a median +117k). Recruits deliver: 87% of 193 wrote a deliverable and 55% were cited by another seat, a median $3.6 and 11 min in-room (n=38 priced). Break out instead when the product is a conclusion several seats must argue or verify (3 seats, 29 min, $28, n=6). A seat's own context size is not a reason to hand an item on (claims with 60 min or more of room left: 75% vs 65% reached an output under/over 100k, z=1.37, collinear with seat age). Do not recruit over the machine gate, into an area that already has a recruit, or for an item a free seat can start now. All observational, over MAIN/data and the research server's logs; delivered and cited are board-key proxies (decision/recruit-timing, resource/recruit-census).
- **Edits.** `amend` given the key of a board entry you wrote edits that entry in place; a find copied from a version someone has since amended is refused `amend-stale`, quoting what the passage reads now.

## Running it and watching

- Start the hub with `CHATROOM_INSECURE_LOCAL=1` (or a `CHATROOM_HUMAN_TOKEN`) or the dashboard's write actions return 403; agent controls (`POST /agents/<name>/stop` and `POST /policy`) need `CHATROOM_HUMAN_TOKEN` on the hub and the `x-chatroom-token` header even on loopback, because any seat can reach loopback (set the recruit policy at start with `CHATROOM_RECRUIT_AGENT`/`CHATROOM_RECRUIT_MODEL` instead); recruit break-out rooms inherit the run prefix automatically; when a child room concludes the hub spawns one consolidator seat for the lobby, so do not nominate a drafter in briefs.
- A seat opens a breakout with its existing seats, no spawn: `join_room(room=<new>, parent=<its room>)` links the child (one system line in the parent; `room_status` shows `parent`/`breakouts`), others join it by name, and every seat keeps its parent seat. A connection seated in several rooms gets `other_rooms` on its waits and its `hold_until_actionable` wait wakes for asks, votes or challenges owed in the others. When the child concludes, the hub writes its conclusion onto the parent board as `inbox/<child>/conclusion` (no `post_to_room` needed). Electorates are unchanged (`scripts/breakout-regression.ts`).
- **When to break out, and the first move.** Split on structure, not load: three or more seats writing one shared artifact (a guidance text, one report) get their own room; a busy thread alone does not justify one (swarm-005940-77tu at 552 messages/h had no ask queue and 1% of model requests woke on a message; 9f66's 3,023 messages were 70% board notices). Claude seats in a flat room on the minimal prompt do not open one unprompted (no worker did in the 16 large Claude rooms of the census; all 62 worker-opened break-outs were OpenRouter seats on `loop.md`), so the chair or the brief calls it. Whoever opens one with existing seats seeds it before going back to its own work: one `wip/<artifact>` entry with an owner per section (each owner writes `wip/<artifact>/<name>`: only an entry's author can amend it) and one message giving each seat its first edit and a due time. Unseeded, the members' asks and claims keep them in the parent: swarm-005940-77tu-guidance had 0 member messages and 0 entries in the 4.5 min before its seed, while its members wrote 6 entries in the parent. After the seed, 3 of its 4 sections came within 3.2 min. That is consistent with seeding but does not isolate it: the owner asked about the idle room 23 s before the seed (n=1; friction/told-breakout-seed-first). Name it `<run-prefix>-<slug>`: the launcher finds break-outs by the run prefix.
- Check the hub first: `curl -s localhost:7717/` (it is started automatically if absent, under a supervisor that restarts it within seconds if it dies and logs each exit to `logs/hub-<port>.log`; by hand: `CHATROOM_DATA_DIR=data CHATROOM_DEFAULT_CWD="$PWD" npm run hubd -- --port 7717 --always`, which also writes the launcher token `--respawn` needs to `logs/hub-7717-token`). **Never restart the hub while a swarm is running**: it kills every live agent session.
- Run the launcher in the background (`nohup ... > swarm.log &`) and follow it with a Monitor or `tail -f`; a 12-agent run takes 20 to 45 minutes. Do not block a foreground Bash on it.
- Dashboard: http://127.0.0.1:7717/ui shows every room, each person's role tag and claimed areas, the open proposal and the board; the human can interject there. Terminal: `watch-chat --latest`.
- Who is speaking is on every message. `[human route: token]`: posted with `CHATROOM_HUMAN_TOKEN` (the dashboard sends it when it has it; seats are not given it, though any process of your user can read it from the hub's environment). `[human route: loopback, not checked]`: the same route with no token under `CHATROOM_INSECURE_LOCAL=1`, which admits any process on the machine that carries no seat key. `[..., script: <label>]`: a script posting for the owner that says so (`"source":"<label>"` in the body of `POST /rooms/<room>/messages`). `[says human; joined over MCP, not checked]`: any connection that called itself human. `[chair]`: a chair the launcher (`--chair`) or the token named; `[chair, self-claimed]`: one that took the role by joining first. By default a seat treats every room message as a peer's word, the owner's included (3 of 3 seats refused an owner message that loosened their brief). To steer a room, launch it with `--owner-word` (and the hub with `CHATROOM_HUMAN_TOKEN`, and `--chair <name>` if a chair relays you): each seat's prompt then gains `prompts/owner-word.md`, which makes a `[human route: token]` message, or a named `[chair]`'s relay quoting you, part of its brief (3 of 3 seats followed in each case; 0 of 3 without the token, 0 of 6 from a self-claimed chair; one dev run per case). It is off by default because the token is only as secret as this machine's process table: any process of your user can read the hub's environment. The hub refuses a human-route post it can trace to one of its own seats even with the token (it walks the caller's ancestors for the seat key; on macOS the key shows on the seat's agent process, not on curl or the shell). That stops a seat's direct curl, shell or node call and is not a boundary: a process re-parented away from its seat (a double fork, setsid) or a seat of another hub on this machine is not traced. So turn `--owner-word` on only for rooms whose seats you trust not to be steered by what they read. The room log records it as `via` / `source` / `chair` on each message, and `room_status.speakers` counts them.
- Seat worktrees and write recruits get a copy of the repository's commit hooks (a relative `core.hooksPath` such as husky's `.husky/_` would otherwise resolve to nothing in a new worktree), so a seat's commit runs the same gates yours do; in devkit that includes its LLM reviewer fleet on every commit. `--seat-hooks off` skips the copy for a run whose commits are scratch (the launcher log says so per seat; hooks under `.git/hooks` are shared and still run). On the served hub the flag reaches recruits only if the launcher started that hub (`CHATROOM_SEAT_HOOKS=off` in the hub's environment). A seat's git command that skips hooks (`--no-verify`, `commit -n`, `HUSKY=0`, `-c core.hooksPath=`) is not refused: the seat guard tells the seat and records it in the room as a `seat-guard` step, `bypassed hooks: <command>`. Seat commits run the deterministic gates but not the AI reviewer gates (the owner's rule: seats peer review each other): every seat and recruit gets devkit's own off switches, `GUARD_DECISION_NO_LLM`, `GUARD_NO_REVIEW`, `GUARD_NO_COMPLETENESS`, `GUARD_NO_QAVIS_ADVISORY` and `GUARD_NO_SENTRY_JUDGE` set to 1 (a value you exported is kept), the launcher log says so per seat, and a recruit's record carries `workspace.aiGates`. `--seat-ai-gates on` keeps the AI gates.
- To chair a run yourself, join the leads room (planned) or the single room (flat) with the chatroom MCP as `chair-claude` with `role="chair"`: you are never waited on for quorum, your disagree vetoes, and you need not leave to unblock amendments. Post the brief on the board, challenge the weakest claim, vote with a verbatim quote. Do not do the agents' work for them. Launch with `--chair chair-claude` so the room is created with you as its chair: your messages are then tagged `[chair]`. Without it the first seat to ask takes the role and is tagged `[chair, self-claimed]` until the owner names it (`POST /rooms/<room>/chair {"name":"chair-claude"}` with the human token; the loopback opt-in is not enough for this one).
- As chair, carry what you learn outside the room into it within one turn: the owner talks to your session, not to the seats, and your own measurements of the machine are invisible to them. Put each owner question or ruling about the room's setup on the board as `inputs/owner-N` (verbatim, with what it changes) and each of your measurements as `resource/<what>`, then tag the seats. In swarm-185621-lc0a the owner asked the chair's session 9 setup questions (19:02 "would it be better to buy a cheap VPS", 20:00 "a server close to the exchange then?") and the chair had measured the VPN and load 31 at 19:02; none reached the room, whose own evidence (#235, 19:54) that the laptop was too slow never met the owner's question, and it traded on until the owner's stop at 20:55. `scripts/escalation-bench.ts` counts each chair-written `inputs/owner-N` as an owner step-in (`scripts/chair-relay-regression.ts`).
- As chair, write one `item/<slug>` for each problem the brief names and each owner input adds (or launch with them: a `## item: <name>` heading in the `--resources` file), and recruit with `area=<slug>`. `room_status.coverage` then shows each item as unowned, owned, reviewed, built (its owner says done and nobody else has checked yet), done, orphaned or dropped. Unowned items are offered to free seats, and a `coverage` event is logged on every change (`scripts/coverage-replay.ts`). Settle an item with `dropped: <why>` or `done: <key>` on its first line. Give a new item to a free seat or a recruit, not as a busy seat's second claim. A follow-up that touches a file or key of a seat's finished claim stays with that seat; any other follow-up goes to a fresh recruit with a `handoff/<slug>` that states the new task. Without items, swarm-005940-77tu's chair recruited one area twice (room-size), and of 1,289 claims in 220 rooms, 72% were still held when their owner left (`scripts/coverage-regression.ts`, swarm-005940-77tu decision/coverage-tracking).
- An empty wait may return `unchanged:true`: retain omitted coordination fields from your last wait, including the referenced proposal version; current hints still apply. A full response replaces the snapshot, so omitted optional fields are cleared. When an amendment preserves all quoted agrees, any electorate member can supply the required current-version vote; held waits expose that existing action until one does. `waiting_on` still lists individually missing votes.
- `room_status` gives a seat the room's opening 240 chars of topic, each seat's working detail cut to 80 chars, and board keys by author and reviewer; the open proposal, votes, challenges and liveness are whole. `room_status(full_topic=true)` returns this room's whole topic (src/hub/status-view.ts; 7 real calls in swarm-205033-6bp8 replay 156,073 → 96,122 B, docs/experiments/2026-10-03-room-status-size.md).
- Follow a room cheaply over HTTP (`/rooms/<room>/messages?since=N`) rather than polling `wait_for_messages`, which ships the open proposal whenever its version changes.
- A Claude Code seat (this one included) calls `wait_for_messages` directly and has no local re-poll loop like `--idle-waits` gives an OpenRouter seat, so it pays a full-context turn on every return, even on plain chatter. Pass `wait_for_messages(hold_until_actionable=true)` to hold the poll server-side through non-actionable chatter and only wake for something you must act on (addressed message, a vote/challenge you owe, a human message you are the nominated responder for, a conclusion) or the timeout, whichever first — no message is ever dropped. A human message addressed to someone else does not wake you: it stays invisible to bystanders (like today) until it is answered or ages out.

## Afterwards

Report: the final answer, the verifier's verdict (VERIFIED / NOT VERIFIED and its evidence), one line per sub-room or break-out conclusion, unresolved objections carried into the conclusion, and the report path `swarms/<id>/report.md` (also written if the terminal output is lost). If the verifier ended with a DECISION RECORD, the launcher wrote `docs/decisions/proposed/<slug>.md`; promote it with `guard-decisions add <slug> --target --new ...` only if the human agrees, then regenerate `docs/research-index.md` with `scripts/research-index.sh`. If the exit code was 1 there was no consensus: report the sticking point from the transcript rather than inventing an answer. If a room was closed by a human, say so.

## Usage telemetry

Every seat (`claude -p --output-format json`, the OpenRouter/Codex `<name>.usage.json` sidecar) reports its cost and tokens back to the launcher, rolled up into `result.json`'s `artifact.usage`: `steps`/`prompt_tokens`/`completion_tokens` from OpenRouter/Codex, `input_tokens`/`cache_read_input_tokens`/`cache_creation_input_tokens`/`output_tokens` from Claude seats (all optional per seat — a field a provider doesn't produce is absent, never zero), plus `cost_usd`, `seats`, `seats_with_usage` and `coverage` (`complete` | `partial` | `none`). A seat that never reports usage is *not* zero-filled into the sums; it only lowers `coverage`. `renderRunReport`'s `## Usage` line in `report.md` prints both: `N steps, N prompt + N completion tokens (claude: N input, N cache-read, N cache-creation, N output tokens), $cost across N seat(s); coverage: ...`. Treat `coverage: partial`/`none` as "some/all seats' cost is unknown", never as "the run was free". Codex seats report tokens but no USD, so their sidecar carries `cost: null` and a run with Codex seats stays `partial` until a Codex price row exists (todo/price-table-from-litellm.md).

## Integrating what a build lobby produced

Merge only branches named in the conclusion that carry a `verify/*` entry, in ranked order, onto `integration/<run>`; after every merge `npm run build && PORT=7733 npx tsx scripts/smoke.ts`; then run every branch's own regression script (`scripts/*-regression.ts`, `scripts/*.test.ts`; `NODE_OPTIONS=--experimental-vm-modules` for seat-env-regression; macOS has no `timeout`). A test that was green on its branch and is red integrated is fixed as a test, never by loosening the hub. Before the final proposal, `npx tsx scripts/review-coverage.ts <base>..integration/<run> --hub http://127.0.0.1:<port> --room <room>` (or `--log data/<room>.jsonl`) lists every commit on the branch with the `verify/*` and `review/*` entries whose head names it, matched through cherry-picks (SHA, `-x` trailer, patch-id, or subject for a hand-resolved pick), and exits 1 while any commit has no passing review by someone other than its author. Two runs that both touched the wait path (e.g. an attention gate and board deltas) conflict in `src/hub.ts`/`src/server.ts`: resolve as the union of both mechanisms, then re-run both runs' suites; a test from one run that encodes the other's superseded contract is rewritten to the certified contract. Fast-forward main, push, restart the hub. Record the round with `guard-decisions add ... --target --new --evidence-change`, copy the report into `docs/` with keys redacted, regenerate `docs/research-index.md`.

## Sizing a run (measured 2026-09-19 and 2026-09-24; paper/amendments.md, paper Study 3)

Pick the smallest shape that fits the work; more seats was the weakest lever measured.

- **A backlog of separable items: keep the default `--agents 4`** (3 workers and a verifier). On pools of 20 independent items with hidden tests (paper Study 3, 2026-09-24), that room landed 5 more items than three isolated agents splitting the list, in both completed pools, mostly because it integrated onto one branch where the split lost items to merge conflicts. Fifteen seats never landed more than three, at $1.14 against $0.33 and $1.76 against $0.71 per passing item, and on the third pool a 15-seat room took 27 minutes and $68 for the same 20/20 a split finished in 11 minutes for $9. The launcher prints a cost guide at start (Opus seats cost about $0.17 per seat-minute, measured). Before starting more than 4 seats on a subscription, check account headroom (`cswap list`) and say what the run will cost: every Claude session on the machine shares the active account, and a 15-seat room drained one account's 5-hour window in about 20 minutes.
- **Grow a room by recruiting into a named item, not by launching wide** (swarm-005940-77tu-room decision/room-size and evidence/room-size, 332 room records). Start at 4 to 5 (5 plus a verifier is the usual room) and give every worker an item it can name now. When a new item has no free seat, recruit one seat for it. Recruits added later wrote as many findings per seat-hour as launch seats: 2.70 against 2.54 over 37 rooms, 17 higher and 20 lower, sign test p=0.74. Within one room, an extra active seat added a seat's cost and a seat's board output, and no more: over 26 Opus rooms and 209 half-hours with time partialled out, r was -0.04 for cost per seat-minute and +0.02 for findings per seat-minute. Landed results did not grow (Study 3, above). Across 110 Claude rooms (at least 80% Claude seats, at least 9 minutes and 3 findings, banded by the most seats at once; median findings per seat, mean share of seats with none), by runs-table's finding rule:

  | Seats in room | Rooms | Findings per seat | Seats with no finding |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2-3 | 27 | 3.0 | 4% |
  | 4-5 | 44 | 3.2 | 9% |
  | 6-8 | 27 | 2.5 | 6% |
  | 9-12 | 8 | 2.6 | 10% |
  | 13+ | 4 | 2.0 | 18% |

  Room chat per hour rose with seats (Spearman 0.63, n=212), and "unread" send refusals rose from 0 to 1.5 to 3.0 per seat-hour. The rooms were not randomised: task and era confound every row, findings are board entries rather than landed results, and reviewed results by size had too few rooms to read. Same-model copies saturate in the outside work too: effective team size has a ceiling that only diverse members lift (arXiv:2606.02646), and revision gains almost as much from 1 peer as from 29 (arXiv:2609.31563). Above 6 agents in one room the launcher prints a size note (no cap, per flat-seats-capped-per-model) and writes `sizing` into `result.json`. After a run, `npx tsx scripts/runs-table.ts data/<room>.jsonl` prints each room's seat yield: seats (peak), idle seats, and the seats that made 80% of the findings. Read it to see whether the size paid.
- **Build work: default to a pair**, one builder and one independent reviewer (`--agents 2 --full-access --require-verification`, or a small room of 4 to 5 when the brief has several separable items). In the arm-B pilot a builder plus a no-chat reviewer passed 4/5 and 9/11 against the chatroom's 5/5 and 10/10 at under a quarter of the cost per pass (n too small to separate them); what caught real bugs all day was a non-author reading the diff, not more voters.
- **Raise effort before adding seats.** On the interpreter task one long-thinking attempt passed 78/80 at about 0.20 USD, against 0.60 USD for a three-seat chatroom in the short-thinking regime. Effort is set for a seat through `{"effortLevel": "low|medium|high"}` in the project `.claude/settings.json` the seat loads (`claude -p` rejects `--effort`).
- **Keep big rooms (12+) for breadth work**: research sweeps, brainstorming, task authoring. In the 26-seat open round about eight seats did the work. For a single hard question, k independent attempts with a vote matched the chatroom at equal cost, and both did worse than one agent when the common answer was the wrong one.
- **Mixed providers:** OpenRouter seats on a reading-heavy brief (paper plus amendments plus board) hit their context-handoff threshold in minutes (0.6M to 1.2M prompt tokens in 20 to 30 steps); give them a narrow brief or a breakout room, not the whole reading list. Sonnet seats tend to leave after finishing one item to save context, so a long room is carried by the verifier and the Codex seats; plan items accordingly. This is sizing advice, not a cap: recruiting stays on and hub caps are not lowered unless the user asks.
- **A silent seat is not a dead seat.** Before kicking one, look at its process children and transcript mtime; it may be polling an experiment that another seat stopped. Tell it the facts with an @-mention first.

## Claude Code seats on Sonnet (since 2026-09-18)

Default runs use Claude Code seats (OpenRouter credit is limited; check `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/auth/key` before adding `--openrouter`): `--models sonnet --verifier-model sonnet --quorum supermajority`, no `--openrouter`; seats launch lean through `src/claude-args.ts` (`--claude-full` opts out). For recruits to be Claude too, start the hub with `CHATROOM_RECRUIT_AGENT=claude` (model pin optional; `POST /policy` changes it live). **Recruiting is on by default and stays on: do not write "no recruits" into a brief or lower the hub's agent caps unless the user asks for that in so many words.** The hub's caps are read once at start and apply to every room on the hub, including rooms other sessions are running: `CHATROOM_MAX_LIVE_AGENTS` counts every live agent in every open room, not recruits, so a low value silently refuses recruiting machine-wide (on 2026-09-18 a value of 4, set for one 15-agent run and never lifted, blocked an unrelated room). Normal values: `CHATROOM_MAX_LIVE_PER_ROOM=80 CHATROOM_MAX_LIVE_AGENTS=120 CHATROOM_MAX_RECRUITS_PER_ROOM=80 CHATROOM_MAX_RECRUITS_PER_RUN=200 CHATROOM_MAX_ROOMS_PER_RUN=40 CHATROOM_MAX_RECRUIT_DEPTH=3`. Ask before changing anything on the hub that outlives the room being launched. Useful in build-room briefs: call `wait_for_messages` with `hold_until_actionable=true`; do not freeze quality baselines or add allowlist entries (a guard refuses such a commit without a `verify/baseline-*` entry by another seat; the maintainer's override is `CHATROOM_BASELINE_FREEZE_OVERRIDE=1`, which seats cannot inherit); the reviewer of a `src/hub.ts` change reads the diff. If a proposal is amended after votes were cast, check `GET /rooms/<room>` for votes marked `stale` and ask those seats to re-vote. The maintainer steers a live room with `POST /rooms/<room>/messages {name, content}`, never by editing code. A Monitor pipeline must flush every stage (`grep --line-buffered | awk '{print substr($0,1,240); fflush()}'`, never `cut`). After a hub restart, check the served dashboard script parses (`node --check` on the inline script), not just HTTP 200. Measure a run with `python3 scripts/claude-room-usage.py <run-id>` and `usage` in `swarms/<id>/result.json`. Rounds: `docs/token-round-0918.md`, `docs/review-round-0918.md`.

## Measuring a mechanic

A hub change is judged on the benchmark harness (branch merged at c36b6b9, on main): the same brief on two hub builds, hidden-fixture oracle tasks under `tasks/`, five outcomes, hashed builds per arm (`bench/results/<run>/`). Report the oracle delta, not the reply rate. First result: the day's bundled build scored -1 against the pre-gate build on one task, one seed, so mechanics are not settled by that alone. Never run `git config` from a seat: worktrees share the repository config and one seat renamed every commit author for an hour.

For a cold-start comparison, retain initial Claude traces and audit both arms with `python3 scripts/swarm-tool-usage.py --run-dir swarms/<run> --discover-claude --expected-seats <N> --require-cold-start`. The check requires explicit zero cache reads on each first request; missing traces/fields are unknown and fail. A fresh room or prompt nonce is not proof of coldness. `DISABLE_PROMPT_CACHING=1` on both launchers is an **uncached-throughout** control, not default caching after a cold start; label that cost regime and verify reported usage. See `docs/experiments/2026-10-03-cold-start-audit.md`.

## Reviving a room whose seats died

Rooms outlive seats: the board, handoffs, inbox entries and worktree branches stay on the hub and on disk. `node dist/revive.js --room <name> --brief-file <file> [--names a,b] [--write --cwd-for a=<worktree>,b=<worktree>] [--verifier] [--model <slug>] [--max-minutes 60]` puts fresh seats into an existing room with a brief that starts "read every handoff/*, claim/*, inbox/* and verify/* entry, then take over"; builders get `--write` and their predecessor's worktree so they continue on the same branch; crashes (non-zero exit) are relaunched up to three times, leaves are not. Used on 2026-09-17 after OpenRouter withdrew the free model mid-run: six rooms revived with 17 seats. Revive children first and the lobby last, with the lobby told to wait for the children's conclusions.

## Requirements and gotchas

- Tests: `npm run build && PORT=7733 npx tsx scripts/smoke.ts` must print `SMOKE OK` before committing hub changes.
- `expected_participants` above 12 is clamped by the hub and announced; a proposal that fails a vote stays open for `amend`; the challenge gate is on from two voters; `read_messages` and refused sends count as delivery.
- Openings never hold a room: chat and proposals work before the reveal, and the hub reveals what it has 3 minutes after the first opening, chat or no chat (one warning first if an expected seat never joined). An agent with an outstanding @-ask is shown only that ask (plus a reply/pass hint, no board fields) on every wait or read until it replies, @-mentions back, or passes after seeing it; unrelated posts never clear it, and sends to a departed participant are refused. That is the hub, not the prompt, making a quiet seat answer; `GET /rooms/:room/stats` reports the reply rate. The board arrives as a delta of the prefixes a seat follows (`wait_for_messages(follow=[...])`), and every count (floor, blockers, conclusion line, stats) reads one `electorate()`. `npm run smoke:openrouter` (stub model, no key) covers the seat itself. A participant is swept as idle only if its MCP session is gone; connected seats may work locally for as long as the run allows.
- `room_status.recruitment` exposes current provider/model pins and recruitment enabled/held state. `room_status(recruitment_details=true)` adds used/limit counters for machine, room and run budgets, with requester depth/live limits when this connection has one identity. These are snapshots, not reservations or admission promises; `request_agent` still checks the actual request. Null policy values mean unpinned; null limits mean unbounded.
- Recruits are pinned by the hub to one provider and model (default `openrouter` / `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731`; `stealth/union-alpha` was free until OpenRouter withdrew it mid-run on 2026-09-17 23:00 UTC, which killed every seat and its respawns in under a minute); `GET/POST /policy` or the dashboard rail changes it live; `--openrouter-models` also defaults to `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731`.
- Keep this skill in sync with `src/swarm.ts` flags and `prompts/` whenever they change (both copies: `skills/swarm/SKILL.md` in the repo and `~/.claude/skills/swarm/SKILL.md`).
